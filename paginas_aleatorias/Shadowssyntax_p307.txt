far from some variants of pluralism that both Putnam and Hirsch tend
to focus on.
Putnam and Hirsch quickly go beyond the mere claim of Quantifier
Pluralism and move to an egalitarian version of pluralism, according
to which some of the distinct quantifier meanings are equally good
from an objective point of view. In fact, they both build this condition
into their basic terminology, and thus have no term solely for either
the metasemantic view of quantifiers on an inegalitarian brand of
pluralism. To illustrate, Putnam says of conceptual relativity:
…conceptual relativity always involves descriptions which are
cognitively equivalent ... but which are incompatible if taken at
face value…38
Putnam’s notion of cognitive equivalence is discussed in an earlier
paper of his.39 There he says that two theories are cognitively
equivalent just in case they are mutually relatively interpretable in a
way that preserves goodness of scientific explanation.40 Though
Putnam doesn’t discuss it, this inter-theory notion of equivalence can
be used to define an inter-language notion of equivalence by saying
that two languages are equivalent just in case for every theory
formulable in one language there is a cognitively equivalent theory
