9.7 Using theorems
Every sentence of our logic is, in semantic terms, one of three kinds. It is either
a tautology, a contradictory sentence, or a contingent sentence. We have already
defined “tautology” (a sentence that must be true) and “contradictory sentence” (a
sentence that must be false). A contingent sentence is a sentence that is neither a
tautology nor a contradictory sentence. Thus, a contingent sentence is a sentence
that might be true, or might be false.
