Microworld
A simulated “toy world” in which a few objects and spaces
are modeled and certain aspects of the real world are
ignored. Microworld-based systems have been developed
(particularly in the 1970s and 1980s) to demonstrate that a
certain approach is effective at a general, high level.
Systems that are cognitive models or are focused on
discourse are generally not considered, by virtue of this
type of modeling, to have microworlds. The term is usually
reserved for those that at least simulate where things are
and what actions are taken, if not further details of physics.
Examples: SHRDLU; Adventure; Zork; Novel Writer; TALESPIN; MINSTREL; GESTER; TAILOR; MEXICA; FABULIST;
Dwarf Fortress Legends; Sheldon County.
NaNoGenMo
National Novel Generation Month was at first a glib
comment by author/programmer Darius Kazemi. He
suggested that instead of enduring the rigors of the
NaNoWriMo (National Novel Writing Month), which
happens in November, the computationally inclined should
use that month to do computer-generated work. The idea of
NaNoWriMo is that US writers should produce 50,000
words of novelistic writing. The goal is not to find a
publisher or even polish one's work by revising it, but to
get it all out. Those taking up the challenge of NaNoGenMo
should not write but generate a novel of that length. And to
be more precise, any textual output this long will do—the
concept of the novel is extremely flexible. In addition to the
technical requirement that output be 50,000 words long,
output and code must be freely shared online. And, finally,
there is a process constraint: All the work is supposed to be
done in November. Very many outputs in this book,
