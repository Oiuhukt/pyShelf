When talking about sales later in the week the question
comes up, what’s an effective way to learn about a prospective customer? Somebody will recall the initial get-acquainted
exercise that proved so fruitful: asking about one’s family,
occupation, recreation, and enjoyment. That icebreaker now
morphs into a handy tool for getting to know a prospective
client and it gets an acronym: FORE.
Throughout the week the four principal training topics are
repeatedly touched on, a point is made, and the exercises shift
to related questions. In one session, participants brainstorm
what kinds of marketing and development strategies might
generate the flow of leads they need in order to meet sales targets. An effective sales and marketing system has a structure
called 5-4-3-2-1. Five new business marketing initiatives every month, four cross-marketing and four retention programs
in place, three appointments scheduled every day, two appointments kept (prospects often have to reschedule), one new
customer sold on average two policies per sale. At twenty-two
working days a month, that’s about five hundred new policies
in a year, making twenty-five hundred over the five-year horizon of the agent’s vision.
