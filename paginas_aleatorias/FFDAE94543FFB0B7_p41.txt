Stroud (2018) emphasizes that one can be committed to the truth of a law and maintain
that no justification can be given to it. But this attitude—which Stroud presents as his own—
can hardly be attributed to Frege. This would amount to saying that Frege, for a time,
thought that basic law V was true, and that the question as to how a subject can be justified
in believing basic law V is unanswerable: one cannot say what would make a subject
justified in believing it. But this suggests that Frege recognized no objective criteria for
accepting or rejecting basic law V, which is plainly false. Upon receiving Russell’s letter,
Frege seems to have formed the belief that basic law V is false. Therefore, he recognized at
least one necessary condition for accepting it—namely, if a thought is a basic law, then it
entails no contradiction.
One could reply that this observation only shows that basic law V was not basic after all.

entails—given weak normativity as a bridge principle—that one must reject thoughts that
one knows to be contradictory. I am sceptical about this, because I am inclined to take Frege
