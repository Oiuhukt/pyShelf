Granted, few of the authors using such terminology are likely to think that what
ChatGPT is doing is literally similar to what humans are doing when they are hallucinating or bullshitting. Given the charitable interpretation of this terminology we
adopt in this article, are there reasons to believe that the mental metaphors under consideration are successful? To answer this question, we will first consider conceptual
metaphor theory (Sect. 4) and then discuss in detail the widely used hallucination and
bullshitting metaphors (Sect. 5).

4 Conceptual metaphor theory
To be in a position to describe and evaluate the different uses of metaphorical language to discuss chatbot mistakes, we first need to better understand what metaphors
are, how they work, and how they shape our thinking across scholarly and public
discourses. To this end, we use conceptual metaphor theory introduced by Lakoff and
Johnson (2003) as a framework.9

source domain and a target domain, as well as a mapping between the two (Lakoff &
Johnson, 2003). The mapping takes items from the source domain and maps them to
